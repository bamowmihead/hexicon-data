## In his words

**description:** **Sound:** Creaking and groaning of living roots shifting under the weight of the forest above. Dripping water. Distant scratching. Sound carries strangely — echoes off root walls in unpredictable directions. **Smell:** Rich damp earth, fungal must, root sap. Occasionally the sharp ammonia of creature dens. **Color:** Darkness. Where bioluminescent creatures are present, brief flickers of cold light against the warm browns and pale whites of exposed root surfaces. Root bark ranges from pale birch-white on young roots to dark knotted brown on ancient ones. **Light:** Near total darkness. No sunstone, no glow vines. The only light comes from bioluminescent creatures. A torch illuminates root columns and hanging rootlets creating complex shadows that move as the roots shift. **Verticality:** Varies dramatically. One story tall under the Wetland — crawling and crouching. Two stories under the Amberwood — walking but claustrophobic. Four stories under the Deep Amber — root cathedrals with dangling rootlets like chandeliers and root trunks thicker than buildings. The Undergrove is not a geological cave — it is a **biological basement** created by the root architecture of Caldera's trees. The roots grow so deep and so thick that they create structural voids between them. Life fills those voids. The ceiling is not rock — it is root. The walls are root. The columns are root. Everything is alive and slowly moving. The character changes based on what grows above: **Under the Wetland** — cramped, waterlogged, one story. Root cellar energy. Water seeps everywhere. Moss Roller aggregation sites. Bog Stalker-treated water pools. **Under the Amberwood** — two stories, denser root columns from the tightly packed trees above. Gilt Weaver root penetration. Creeping Tide highways between cavern entrances. **Under the Deep Amber** — four-story root cathedrals. Massive root trunks create pillared halls. Bore Weasel tunnel networks thread through the roots. Moss Roller daytime dormancy sites near weeping vine aquifer connections. Gnomish ruins built into the root architecture. **Root hovels at tree bases** — natural gaps where major roots spread from the trunk. Some large enough to walk into, others require crawling. Cacklers use these to access their lairs. **Hollowed tree tops leading down** — Old Verdant Firs with bore holes and natural hollows that connect the canopy all the way down through the trunk into the Undergrove below. **Burrows straight down** — vertical drops through soil into root voids. Some natural, some dug by wildlife. Easy to fall into if the moss covering them collapses. **Living Architecture** — the roots shift slowly over years. Passages open and close on long timescales. A route that worked last season may be narrower or blocked. The Undergrove is never static. **Gnomish Ruins** — the Gnomes built their civilization into the root architecture of the Deep Amber Undergrove. Root-carved halls, sap-sealed chambers, stairways spiraling around major root trunks. Much of it collapsed or repurposed by goblinoids. Exploring these ruins is a major draw for scholars and treasure seekers. **Cackler Lairs** — the Cackler packs make their dens in the Undergrove, accessible through root hovels at the surface. A Cackler lair encounter in the Undergrove is far more dangerous than meeting them above ground — tight quarters, their territory, and the Matriarch nearby. **Highway Network** — many surface creatures use the Undergrove to travel between areas without crossing dangerous surface territory. Following a well-worn root tunnel might take you somewhere faster than the surface route — or into something's den. *Needs unique Undergrove plant species — root-parasitic, darkness-adapted, fungal.* *Needs unique Undergrove creature species that live here and nowhere else.* Creatures from other biomes that pass through or shelter in the Undergrove: * **Moss Rollers** — daytime dormancy aggregations in the Deep Amber Undergrove near aquifer connections * **Bore Weasels** — tunnel networks thread through roots, using the Undergrove as highways * **Creeping Tides** — emerge from cavern systems through the Undergrove into the Amberwood at night * **Cacklers** — lair and nest in the Undergrove, hunt on the surface * **Stalker Bats** — roost in dry sections during the day
